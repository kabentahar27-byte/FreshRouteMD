2. COMPLETE THE CORE WORKFLOW

The main experience must connect:
User-owned ingredients → equipment-compatible meal → missing ingredients → grocery spending plan.

Food-resource discovery should support this workflow without pretending that a resource has particular ingredients available.

A user must be able to:
- Enter or select ingredients they actually have.
- Select their cooking equipment.
- Set relevant dietary preferences and allergies.
- See compatible recipe suggestions.
- Understand which ingredients they have and which they still need.
- Choose a recipe and adjust servings when supported by reliable recipe quantities.
- Transfer missing ingredients to a grocery list.
- Enter their own grocery prices and quantities.
- See the grocery total and remaining budget or amount over budget.
- Find potentially useful food resources separately.
- Save or print a clear plan and a resource visit checklist.

Do not require a provider inventory feed for recipes based on ingredients entered by the user.

Clearly distinguish:
- Ingredients entered by the user.
- Ingredients required by a recipe.
- Ingredients on a shopping list.
- Availability reported by a provider, if a genuine feed exists.
- Unknown availability.

Never automatically mark a missing ingredient as acquired because a resource was selected or a directions link was opened.

3. IMPLEMENT A TRUSTWORTHY RECIPE SYSTEM

Inspect the existing recipe collection and logic before reusing them. Do not automatically reactivate incomplete demo recipes.

Use a small, carefully reviewed recipe collection rather than a large unreliable one.

Each published recipe must have:
- A name and short description.
- Defined ingredients with usable quantities and units.
- A stated base serving count.
- Required equipment.
- Complete, coherent preparation steps.
- Preparation time labeled appropriately as an estimate.
- Applicable dietary and known allergen information.
- Honest attribution or provenance when using third-party recipe material.

Do not copy copyrighted recipe instructions without appropriate permission. Use original instructions or appropriately licensed material and preserve required attribution.

Equipment logic must:
- Require all equipment needed for one supported preparation method.
- Support alternative methods only when each has valid equipment requirements and corresponding instructions.
- Exclude or clearly separate recipes that cannot be prepared with the selected equipment.
- Never count one matching tool as satisfying an entire equipment requirement.
- Respect storage and refrigeration requirements where relevant.

Ingredient logic must:
- Normalize common ingredient names without making unsafe substitutions.
- Distinguish available ingredients from missing ingredients.
- Avoid claiming quantity sufficiency when the user has not entered quantities.
- Scale quantities correctly when servings change.
- Avoid silently converting incompatible units.
- Use deterministic, explainable matching and ranking.

Allergy logic must:
- Exclude recipes containing a known conflicting ingredient when that allergy is selected.
- Treat unknown ingredient or product information as unknown.
- Avoid claiming a recipe is allergy-safe.
- Explain that brands, substitutions, labels, and cross-contact require user verification.
- Avoid medical or nutrition claims that the app cannot support.

Do not publish unsafe or ambiguous cooking instructions. If recipe safety or completeness cannot be established, keep that recipe out of suggestions and report the issue.

4. CONNECT THE SPENDING PLAN

Preserve and improve the current manual budget calculator.

Requirements:
- Transfer missing recipe ingredients into the grocery list through an explicit user action.
- Preserve ingredient names, units, and required quantities.
- Let users edit, remove, combine, and mark items acquired.
- Prevent accidental duplicate entries when transferring the same recipe repeatedly.
- Do not merge incompatible units or distinct products.
- Distinguish recipe quantities from purchasable package quantities.
- Do not silently equate one recipe unit with one grocery package.
- Allow manual prices when no verified price source exists.
- Clearly identify any genuinely supported estimate.
- Never fabricate store prices, savings, promotions, or pantry stock.
- Label totals as incomplete when required prices are missing.
- Do not treat a blank price as a confirmed zero-dollar item.
- Include only explicitly priced grocery quantities in the priced subtotal.
- Explain excluded costs such as taxes, transportation, and fees.
- Use precise currency calculations.
- Validate negative, blank, nonfinite, excessively large, and otherwise invalid values.
- Support realistic quantities and units, including fractional quantities where appropriate.
- Show “Remaining” or “Over budget” using text as well as color.
- Preserve the active plan during language switching, route changes, refreshes, and browser back navigation where technically appropriate.

Persist user-entered plans locally unless the existing application has a supported, authorized account-based storage system. Explain what is stored and provide an explicit way to clear it.

Do not overwrite existing saved data during migration.

5. MAKE FOOD DISCOVERY USEFUL AND HONEST

Preserve the official sourced directory and existing legitimate data integrations.

Clearly distinguish food assistance from grocery retailers:
- SNAP-authorized retailers sell groceries; SNAP acceptance does not mean free food, low prices, WIC acceptance, or particular stock.
- Food-pantry classification does not by itself confirm free service, eligibility, hours, or availability.
- Unknown values must remain visibly unknown.

Resource details should make these facts discoverable:
- Resource name and type.
- Address and approximate distance, when supported.
- Published hours and their limitations.
- Eligibility and visit requirements.
- Cost information, when supplied.
- Contact options.
- Original sources and relevant dates.
- Accessibility or transportation information only when supported.
- Source conflicts and missing information.

Add a useful visit checklist containing relevant unanswered questions, such as:
- Are you operating on the intended visit date?
- Do you serve my area or household?
- Is an appointment or identification required?
- Are there dietary accommodations?
- What should I bring?
- Is the location accessible?

Do not imply that the checklist answers have been confirmed.

Allow users to save a resource or include it in their plan without claiming that groceries were obtained.

6. REMOVE OR FIX MISLEADING CONTROLS

Audit every visible control.

Each control must either:
- Produce an implemented, useful effect; or
- Be removed from the primary workflow or explicitly identified as unavailable.

Transportation:
- Do not present transportation selection as a working route filter unless it genuinely changes results using supported information.
- Straight-line distance must not be presented as walking, driving, cycling, or transit distance.
- Do not invent travel times or transit accessibility.
- If useful routing cannot be implemented with available supported services, retain approximate-distance search and external directions, and move transportation preferences out of the main search controls.
- Preserve any existing saved transportation preference without implying unsupported behavior.

Personalization:
- Apply dietary preferences, allergies, equipment, and ingredient selections to recipes where supported.
- Apply preferences to resource results only when actual source fields support doing so.
- Do not infer dietary accommodations from a resource’s name or category.
- Remove unsupported match scores.
- Prefer concise explanations of the actual factors used.

Navigation:
- Keep Find food, Choose a meal, and Plan your spending as the primary tasks.
- Ensure each destination performs its advertised task.
- Audit Community, Learn, About, Profile, the sourced directory, and provider-related destinations.
- Give duplicate destinations a distinct useful purpose or consolidate their navigation while preserving useful direct links.
- Do not keep a separate destination that merely disguises the finder as another completed feature.
- Preserve browser back behavior and direct links.

7. FIX DATA REFRESH AND FAILURE BEHAVIOR

Inspect the current periodic refresh implementation.

Required behavior:
- Background refresh must not close an open resource detail panel.
- Preserve the selected resource by stable identity when possible.
- If a selected resource changes or disappears, explain what happened without abruptly discarding the user’s context.
- A transient source failure must not erase the last successful results.
- Keep the last successful retrieval available with a clear timestamp and stale-data notice.
- Distinguish failed loading from a legitimate empty result.
- Provide Retry.
- Avoid overlapping requests and prevent older responses from overwriting newer state.
- Clean up timers and requests when appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessary complete dataset downloads every minute.
- Choose a refresh and caching strategy appropriate to the actual sources, their update frequency, and their documented limits.

Clearly distinguish:
- Time the app last fetched the source.
- Time the source reports being updated.
- Time a provider last confirmed information.
- Inventory confirmation, which remains unknown without genuine inventory data.

A successful API request is not verification that an organization is operating today.

Do not implement scheduled stock updates unless a real, authorized provider data path exists. Automatic timestamps or repeated directory requests must never masquerade as stock tracking.
